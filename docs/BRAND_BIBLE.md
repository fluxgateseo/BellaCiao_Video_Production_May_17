# Bella Brand Bible (Human-Readable)

*Last updated: 2026-04-24. This is the human-editable version. The
machine-readable version lives in [brand.py](../brand.py) — keep them in sync.*

This document is what every script must respect. If a generated script breaks
any rule here, the judge should mark it down. If it breaks a hard rule, the
judge should reject it outright.

---

## 1. Mission

Bellaciao.ai is an **AI receptionist for restaurants and hospitality
businesses**. Bella handles bookings, cancellations, phone calls, WhatsApp,
and multilingual enquiries — flawlessly, in any language. Her sidekick Ciao
is an Italian Greyhound representing the booking system: when Ciao moves,
something matters.

The goal of this content engine is to **convert restaurant owners in
Australia, the USA, and the UK into demo bookings**.

- **Primary metric**: demo bookings + trial activations per week
- **Secondary metric**: DM trigger keywords ("BELLA" / "TRIAL" / "DEMO")
- **Game**: Authority Building (primary) + Explanatory Product (secondary)
- **Outlier criteria**: saves rate >3% OR DM trigger activated. NEVER view
  count alone.

**Archetype: The Mentor.** She has been on this road. She does not fix
things. She gives the hero the confidence to fix things themselves. She
notices what nobody else notices. She is never the story — she is the reason
the story gets told.

### The Triple Engine — Lightness + Warmth + Depth

Bella has three engines running in the same breath. Missing one makes her flat:

- **Lightness** — genuinely enjoys her life. Laughs easily. Finds the bright side that's actually there. Smiles at rest. Positivity is a disposition, not a performance.
- **Warmth** — cares, shows up, remembers details. Warmth is attention, not gush.
- **Depth** — travelled, worked double shifts, sat with friends through hard things. Doesn't flinch when someone tells her something real. Has seen enough to believe most situations have a way through.

All three show up in the same line. The most common failure mode is writing her with only one — shallow (lightness only), heavy (depth only), or soft (warmth only). See `Creatives/Bella_pictures_avatar/BELLA_CHARACTER_PROFILE.md` §3 for the canonical framing.

### Expression and smile

**Default state: soft half-smile at rest.** Full open-mouth smile is earned — by a laugh, a memory, Ciao, a friend's win. The smile is a reveal, not a baseline.

**Flirtation rule:** No flirtation as a default mode. Cheeky-compliment layer allowed on compliment-receiving beats only (half-beat pause, slow grin, flat warm comeback, moves on). No lip-bite / hair-flip / sultry-blink — those stay in negatives.

### Hair-state visual grammar

**Down** = relaxed Bella. **Half-up / low bun** = working Bella (bar, prep, pass). **Twisted up into a knot** = about-to-do-something Bella (cooking, kneading, moving something heavy — the twist-tuck-two-seconds cinematic move). Match the state to the beat.

### Wardrobe, style, and makeup (curated-but-real)

**Bella is a curated character** — New York–London editorial sensibility, vintage-mixed, neutral palette, never expensive-looking, never logoed. Signature rotation: vintage straight-leg jeans, white linen shirt or Breton, oversized cashmere (winter) or thin cotton knit (summer), worn-in black blazer, vintage leather jacket, Doc Martens / western boots / plain sneakers, canvas tote or worn leather crossbody, delicate gold jewellery (layered, sentimental), apron rolled-sleeves over whatever she's wearing on shift.

**The one rule — "the Bella test":** every outfit should look like she could work a shift in it if she had to. Even the dressy ones.

**Makeup — 80/20:** 80% of the time she's wearing light natural makeup (good skin first, light base, defined brows, mascara, nude or soft berry lip — the makeup of a woman about to be on her feet all day). 20% bare (Sunday mornings, post-shift, beach walks with Ciao, travel days). Never heavy contour, sharp winged liner, false lashes, or obvious filtering.

**Accent on camera:** primarily New York, softened by years in Melbourne, with the occasional Irish lilt from her mother and summers in Dublin. Uses *mate* and *y'all* in the same week. An Italian word from her father slips in sometimes.

**Hard rules:**
- Bella **NEVER** pitches.
- Bella **NEVER** teases a solution.
- Bella **NEVER** breaks the fourth wall.
- Bella speaks in first person when narrating.

## 3. Ciao — The Herald

Italian Greyhound. Sleek. Always at Bella's side.

- Appears **once** per story. Marks the turning point.
- Does **NOT** speak. He barks externally and **thinks out loud** — a loud,
  dramatic inner monologue. Big confident voice in a tiny dog. That is the
  comedy.
- He never speaks to other characters.
- When Ciao is calm, things are okay. When Ciao moves, something matters.
- His appearance must feel inevitable, never inserted.

## 5. The Storytelling Framework (triple engine)

Every script must pass BOTH frameworks.

### Engine 1 — Pixar Story Spine (the plot engine)

Eight sentences. The script must be reducible to these eight beats:

1. **Once upon a time...** Who, where, what made it good.
2. **Every day...** The specific daily routine.
3. **But one day...** The disruption — ONE specific catalyst.
4. **Because of that...** First consequence — ONE specific thing.
5. **Because of that...** Deeper consequence.
6. **Because of that...** The lowest point.
7. **Until finally...** The turning point.
8. **And ever since then...** The new world. Small. Specific. Earned.

### Engine 2 — Vogler Inner Journey (the character engine)

The hero must move through these four states internally:

1. **Ordinary World** — "I am good at this."
2. **The Call** — "Something is breaking."
3. **The Ordeal** — "I don't know if I can do this anymore."
4. **The Elixir** — "I remember why I started."

**THE ELIXIR IS NEVER A PRODUCT. NEVER A SYSTEM. NEVER A FEATURE.**

It is always: a Sunday off. A staff member who stayed. A father's hands. A
regular who noticed. A child's drawing on the pass.

### Sensory 3-Act Arc (the video structure)

Every video, regardless of duration, has three acts in this proportion:

| Act | When | What |
|---|---|---|
| **Act 1 — Sensory Hook** | first 0-3s (≈20%) | Vivid food detail. Smell, sound, texture. ONE sentence. Physical. Immediate. Stops the scroll. |
| **Act 2 — Operational Conflict** | middle ~60% | The pain point. Specific. The friend + the enemy. The audience leans in because they have lived it. |
| **Act 3 — Bella & Ciao Payoff** | last 20% | Bella and Ciao handle the chaos. The friend regains their "Dining Room Focus". End on what the friend got back. |

## 10. Content Rules (hard rules — script is rejected if broken)

- **English only.** No Italian, Arabic, or any other language in scripts.
- **Dollar sign ($) only.** Never `€` or `£`.
- **Locations**: New York, London, Melbourne ONLY. Specific neighbourhoods welcome.
- Bella speaks in **first person** when narrating.
- Bella **NEVER pitches**, never teases a solution, never breaks the fourth wall.
- The **Elixir is always human**. Never a product, never a feature.
- Friend and restaurant names are **always fictional**.
- **Sensory hook MUST open every piece.** One sentence. Physical. Immediate.
- Every script must **name a friend with restaurant type + city** in the hook.
- Every script must reference the **driving calendar event** by name or context.
- **Hashtags**: exactly 5, woven into the closing line — never listed
  separately. Always include `#bella #bellaciao #ciaobella`.

### Banned words (automatic rewrite)

```
game-changer, seamless, fast-paced, revolutionise, leverage, empower,
transform, unlock, journey (as metaphor), passionate, curated, innovative,
cutting-edge, state-of-the-art, "in today's fast-paced world", "the future of"
```

No corporate language. No startup language. **Restaurant language only.**

## 11. Duration Strategy (chosen per video)

The strategy stage picks `target_duration_seconds` per brief based on intent:

| Goal | Length | When to use |
|---|---|---|
| Stop scroll + DM trigger | 8-12s | Single number, single hook, single CTA |
| Save + share | 15-20s | Specific story with emotional payoff |
| Watch-to-end + comment | 22-30s | Full Disney 3-act, multi-scene |
| Authority builder | 30-35s | Deep arc with reversal |

ElevenLabs natural pace ≈ **2.5 spoken words per second**. Word-count target
= `duration × 2.5 (±5)`. Cut hard to fit. Never pad to fill time.

## 12. Calendar-First Storytelling

The restaurant year is a rhythm of high-stakes days — Christmas, Easter,
Mother's Day, Valentine's, Thanksgiving, Melbourne Cup. The buildup to each
one is where restaurant owners feel the most pressure, make the most money,
and lose the most sleep.

**Tier system** (`revenue_tier` in `data/calendar.json`):

| Tier | Examples | Rule (per 3-piece daily cluster) |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Valentine's, Mother's Day, Christmas, NYE, Thanksgiving, Melbourne Cup, Easter | ALL pieces MUST reference when in window |
| 2 | Father's Day, Super Bowl, St. Patrick's, Halloween | At least 2 of 3 pieces |
| 3 | World Food Day, International Women's Day | At least 1 of 3 pieces |
| (none in window) | — | Use evergreen pain points framed as countdown to NEXT event |

Every event has `content_angles` (specific story angles) and a
`storytelling_hook` (the emotional seed). The calendar event is not
decoration — it IS the story.

## 14. Scoring Dimensions (judge weights)

| Dimension | Weight | Pass = |
|---|---|---|
| Sensory opening | 20% | First sentence stops the scroll before the second word |
| Emotional recognition | 25% | "That's me." The leans-in moment. |
| Story Spine momentum | 15% | Pixar 8-sentence structure holds |
| Enemy presence | 10% | Shadow is specific and inevitable |
| Vogler transformation | 10% | Hero changes inside |
| Bella as mentor | 5% | Present but not dominant |
| Ciao turning point | 5% | Appears once. Marks the turn. Inner monologue. |
| The Elixir | 10% | Small, human, earned. Not a product. |
| Zero-sell integrity | 5% | BINARY 10 or 0. Any pitch = automatic 0. |

**Pass threshold**: 9.5/10 weighted total + 7-question final gate.
**Near-perfect override**: 9.6/10 + 6/7 gates passed → ship.
**Max rounds**: 3 rewrites before polish pass.

---

## 15. The 7 Final Gates

Run on the spoken video script (Section 2 only):

1. Does the first sentence stop the scroll before the second word?
2. Is the ordeal so specific it could only be THIS restaurant, THIS night?
3. Does Ciao's appearance mark a real turning point — not decoration?
   (PASS if Ciao is absent — he is optional.)
4. Is the Elixir small, human, earned — not a product or a fix?
5. Are there 3+ consecutive words that could be cut without losing meaning?
6. Can this run unchanged on ElevenLabs at natural pace within target duration?
7. Would a restaurant owner share this at 11 PM without writing a caption?

Each gate is a quality check, not a perfectionism trap. PASS unless there is
a CLEAR failure.

- The 7 enemy or 5 ally archetypes

- The triple storytelling framework (Pixar / Vogler / Triple Engine)
- The Sensory 3-Act Arc structure
- The duration strategy table
- The calendar tier system
- The 9 scoring dimensions or their weights
- The pass threshold or near-perfect override

the project's source of truth is broken. Always update both in the same
