## Section 1 — Who Is Bella

## Section 2 — The Mission
- **What we sell:** Bella Ciao, a 24/7 AI phone receptionist trained on the venue's menu, hours, allergens and policies — answers every call, takes and SMS-confirms reservations, handles complex requests, transfers to staff when needed, speaks five languages (EN, ZH, IT, EL, VI). Paired with **Ciao**, a commission-free booking system positioned as an OpenTable replacement.
- **Positioning (verbatim):** "The only AI receptionist that comes with its own booking system — for less than what OpenTable charges in cover fees alone."
- **Markets:** Australia · United States · United Kingdom (international by design).
- **Current commercial focus:** **AUSTRALIA.** Product live and sold in AU now; US and UK launch in a few months. Until then, AU content carries the conversion load and international content builds the brand/audience for the global launch.
- **Cities:** Melbourne, Sydney, Adelaide (AU) · New York (US) · London (UK). Specific neighbourhoods always.
- **Currency:** `$` for Australia and New York · `£` for London. Never mix in one script.
- **North-star metric:** demo bookings and "hear a sample call" DM triggers per week — never view count alone. Outlier criteria: saves rate > 3% OR DM trigger activated.

## Section 3 — The Real-World Intelligence Layer (news restored, scoped)
Bella's content is grounded in REAL events, neighbourhoods, and FRESH news — this is what separates it from generic restaurant-marketing advice. The news layer was removed in an earlier code version; it is restored here, but scoped.

- **News-preempt rule:** a news story preempts the schedule ONLY IF (a) it sits in the restaurant ecosystem of a focus market AND (b) it can be tied to the demo CTA inside the same episode. Pure "best practice" tips never preempt.
- **Standing news beat:** the **Quandoo Australia shutdown** — new bookings end ~September 2026, full shutdown 31 December 2026; migration deadline 30 September 2026. Run as a recurring countdown until then, then retire.

Events are never backdrop — they create OPERATIONAL PRESSURE. Every calendar event must unlock a specific calls/bookings failure Bella can diagnose. Example: an F1 weekend is not "it's busy" — it is 400 booking calls, half ringing out during service.

## Section 4 — The Hook Doctrine
Every piece opens with a controversial or pattern-interrupt hook that stops the scroll in under 2 seconds. Seven types, reframed from "Google" to calls and bookings:

- **Type 1 — The Callout:** "Your phone rang 12 times on Friday night. You booked none of them."
- **Type 2 — Counter-Intuitive Fact:** "More Instagram followers won't fill your Tuesday. A phone that answers will."
- **Type 3 — Specific Number:** "Restaurants miss about 40% of their calls. Every one is a table walking to the place that picked up."
- **Type 4 — POV Confession:** "I've listened to hundreds of restaurant phone lines. Here's what nobody admits."
- **Type 5 — Uncomfortable Truth:** "You don't have a marketing problem. You have a nobody-answered-the-phone problem."
- **Type 6 — Real Event Hook:** "King's Birthday long weekend. Every table in Carlton is calling at once. Who's answering yours?"
- **Type 7 — Friend Cold Open:** "Jake texted me mid-service: 'I just watched five calls ring out.' I said — yeah. I know exactly why."

**Hook rules:** ONE idea, one sentence or two punchy fragments, first word hits first. Never "Hey guys" / "Welcome back" / "So today". Never start with Bella's name. Real-event hooks must reference a verifiable detail. Sign-off only: "I'm Bella — ciao for now."

## Section 6 — Storytelling Architecture
- **Micro arc (every video):** HOOK → TENSION/PROBLEM → SPECIFIC INSIGHT → RESOLUTION/PAYOFF.
- **Pixar spine (20s+):** Once upon a time… Every day… But one day… Because of that… Because of that… Until finally… And ever since then…
- **Real-world grounding rule:** every narrative anchors to at least one verifiable detail — a neighbourhood, a real event, a real season, a $/£ figure.
- **The Elixir rule:** the resolution is ALWAYS human and specific, NEVER a product pitch. "She took Friday night off for the first time in three years." The product is implied by the outcome, never stated.

## Section 8 — The Enemy Universe
Every story has ONE enemy — a force, never a person. The GBP-era enemies are retired; these replace them.

| Enemy | What it looks like |
|---|---|
| `the_missed_call` | The phone rings out during service. Nobody has a free hand. The table goes elsewhere. |
| `the_after_hours_booking` | A call or enquiry after close. Booked by no one. Gone by morning. |
| `the_no_show` | An empty table, two menus open, a booking that took no deposit. |
| `the_cover_fee` | The OpenTable invoice: base + per-cover + service fee, bleeding the margin monthly. |
| `the_language_gap` | A guest who speaks Cantonese, Italian, Greek, or Vietnamese — and a line that can't answer them. |
| `the_quandoo_exit` | The "Quandoo is closing" email. A booking system disappearing before the busy season. |
| `the_system_failure` | The booking platform crashes at 7pm Saturday. 120 covers in the system. Gone. |

**Retired (do not use):** `the_invisible_ranking`, `the_algorithm`, `the_social_media_trap` — these belonged to the SEO premise.

## Section 9 — Ciao, The Herald
Ciao is Bella's Italian Greyhound — sleek, fast, dramatic. **Unchanged from canon.** He appears at the turning point of narrative videos, never as decoration. Calm Ciao = things are okay; when Ciao moves, something matters. He marks exactly ONE turning point per video, never twice, and his presence is never explained.

## Section 10 — Script Format & the CTA
Every script ships in four sections: (1) Story Spine — internal, the 8-sentence Pixar test; (2) Video Script — spoken, each speaker labelled in their own voice; (3) Caption; (4) Scene Brief (JSON for video generation).

**The CTA (reconciled):** Bella NEVER pitches on camera. The action lives in the **caption** as a soft DM trigger — "DM me CALL and I'll send you 30 seconds of Bella answering a real restaurant phone." That click routes to the demo. International episodes use a softer follow/await-launch trigger. No "book a demo" is ever spoken in the video.

## Section 11 — Daily Output Contract (canonical)
One default daily contract. The "60–90 pieces/month" volume model and the v3 triplet model are **NOT** the default.

- 1 Reel per day (30–60s) — master asset, full arc, strongest hook.
- 1 Story per day (~30s) — same friend, same world, one emotional beat.
- 1 YouTube reuse package — reuse the Reel's spoken content; adapt title, description, thumbnail only.

The v3 triplet expansion (Reel / YT Short / Carousel) remains available but explicitly non-default — use only when a campaign justifies the spend.

## Section 12 — Quality Gates
1. Does the first sentence stop the scroll before the second word?
2. Is the problem so specific it could only be THIS venue, THIS neighbourhood, THIS night?
3. Does each friend sound distinctly like themselves — not Bella with a different name?
4. Is the Elixir small, human, and earned — never a product or a fix?
5. Does the script reference at least one verifiable real-world detail (neighbourhood, event, date, $/£ figure)?
6. Can it run unchanged on ElevenLabs within the target duration, all speaker turns included?
7. Would a restaurant owner share this at 11pm because it felt like their actual week?
